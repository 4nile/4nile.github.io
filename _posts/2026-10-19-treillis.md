I have the pleasure to introduce the Python library I have been developing all year, for the generation and modeling of lattices. It is largely imperfect as it is written by experimental physicists, and will hopefully improve as time goes, but the first version works and I think can open the way to many fun research and collaborations.

> [*treillis*](https://github.com/sphynx-fracture/treillis) (/tʁɛji/, French for truss) is a Python library built to help researchers and whoever is interested in microlattices. It provides tools to modelize 2D and 3D microlattices, to compute their elastic properties as well as calculate stresses, strains and elastic constants.
> It was written by experimental physicists with the language that they know, and may need some optimization work, but aims at being as easy to use and iterate on as possible.


<!--more-->

<h3>Project inception</h3>

The work on *treillis* started in 2019, for [the PhD of Antoine Montiel](https://theses.hal.science/tel-04639079), on the generation of random isotropic lattices. Antoine's work is the foundation for *treillis*. As PhD students often lack the time in their three-years contracts to clean their code, what was then called *PyLat* consisted in a folder full of repeated scripts with small iterative changes written by all the students that had worked on it, and circular imports. I started the work of cleaning and reorganizing it first and foremost for my own PhD student who joined the group just three weeks ago, so that the learning curve would be softer for her than it was for me, and allow her to focus quickly on her own project. On the way, and by talking to colleagues in radically different fields, I realized it would be of great interest to release the library so that people could make their own samples with it, and add on to the code with other scientific fields applied to the lattice architecture.

<h3>What *treillis* does</h3>

*treillis* creates simplistic lattices, represented by essentially two numpy arrays: the `nodes` which are positions in 2D or 3D space, and the `elems` which are the indices of linked nodes, creating the elements of the lattices. A variety of meshes have been implemented, including two isotropic ones: Delaunay-based and Voronoi-based lattices, that directly pertain to my own research. Lattices can be manipulated, nodes moved, elements added or deleted...
A graphic module with two options has been added to be able to visualize the lattices and plot paper-quality figures.
Finally, a mechanics modeling module allows to perform extremely basic FEM-like simulations of linear mechanics experiment and fracture, with three possible element types: random fuse network, springs, or beams (Euler, or Timoshenko theory).

<h3>Possible evolutions</h3>

*treillis* is integrally written by humans, and full of human mistakes. The first step is to continuously debug it. In longer terms, I would like to change its structure to a namespace library, so that modules besides the creation and plotting are optional, to reduce its memory weight, and make the development and implementation of modules in other fields (electronics, turbulence, statistics... whichever is of interest to you!) much easier.

Another point of importance is the installation: because it uses libraries that are either on conda-forge or on pypi, and that do not work properly when going to the othre package manager, the environment needs to be created before *treillis* is installed, which is less than ideal. I have to improve in my understanding of conda package building to allow for seemless installation.

I am mostly alone in writing it as of now, until I have properly trained my Matlab and Fortran-used colleagues to use Python, although their inputs are extremely useful to improve both functionality and the [documentation](https://treillis.readthedocs.io/).

<h3>Open source</h3>

*treillis* is released under an L-GLP3 license, it is open-source, and I would love to hear about your use if you install it, or if you write additionnal submodules based on it. There are no papers associated to it yet, but I would greatly appreciate if you could [cite us](https://github.com/sphynx-fracture/treillis/blob/main/CITATION.cff) if you use it for a publication.
