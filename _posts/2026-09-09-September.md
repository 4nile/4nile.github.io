<h2>Scientific literature</h2>
<h3>A new insight on quantifying the rheology of dense emulsions</h3>
[link](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2611013123){:.button.button--primary.button--pill}

> Chen et al., *PNAS*, 2026

Prestigious soft matter scientists have worked together to improve the models for dense soft matter --high internal pressure emulsions, jammed soft gels... based on the capillary pressure rather than the shear stress. Using a homemade new type of rheometer, they managed to write a unified constitutive law that they hope can be extended to many other dense soft systems.

<h3>Aging of amorphous materials under cyclic strain</h3>
[link](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2515075123){:.button.button--primary.button--pill}

> Shohat et al., *PNAS*, 2025

When mechanically cycled a large number of times, amorphous materials show a logarithmic decay of the energy they dissipate, and this for various extremely different systems (a crumpled sheet, steel wool, glassy alloys...). DIfferent models already exist to try and understand the behavior of amorpous materials, and these clever experiements allowed to discriminate between two, shining light on the key mechanisms behind their dissipative behavior. A network of complex springs thus allows to model effectively the dissipation of amorphous materials.
